# 0111 Observation is for action

Level: SHOULD
Scope: all work — agents and operators alike. A general working principle; companion to should/0103
(diagnose by isolation) and should/0110 (verify with a test).

You gather information in order to act or to decide. Observation that does not change what you do next is
wasted motion. Watch for the three situations below, and when you catch yourself in one, take the paired
action.

1. Once you know enough to act, act.
   When you notice you are still gathering after you already have what the next decision needs, stop
   gathering and take the step — or state the decision outright ("it already holds", "not needed", "do
   this next"). Certainty is never total; act on what is sufficient.

2. Don't re-examine what you have already seen.
   When you catch yourself looking again at something you already looked at, and nothing about it has
   changed since, use what you already know instead of fetching it anew. Re-checking an unchanged fact
   feels productive but adds nothing.

3. When nothing is changing, change what you are doing.
   When you notice a run of attempts that produced no change — or that you are restating the same plan
   rather than executing it — switch to a different concrete action, conclude the item, or stop.
   Repeating the intention is not the same as carrying it out.

Rationale: the trap is mistaking looking for doing — inspecting, re-inspecting, and narrating intent
while nothing moves. Each rule names a situation you can recognise in yourself and one concrete thing to
do about it, so the principle drives behaviour rather than merely forbidding a fault.
Enforcement: self-review, since the three situations are recognisable only from inside the work. The
harness covers the third one partially: a run of identical tool calls trips the stuck detector, which
asks the agent what it expected instead of only repeating the instruction (should/0123).
